Access control connects reception with the rest of gym operations. Each entry requires identifying the member, checking membership validity and recording a result the team can review later.

Before choosing a reader or changing the process, agree on what should happen in the usual case and what reception will do when something does not match.

## Set one source of truth for memberships

If due dates are in a spreadsheet and entries in another screen, staff have to compare both every visit. A shared record reduces that task and helps spot outdated information.

Also decide who can correct a membership and how a manual change is recorded.

## Choose how members identify themselves

A gym can start with a name or document search at reception and add a digital credential later. If you use QR, a rotating code reduces the chance that a shared screenshot remains valid for long.

The method should be quick for staff and accessible to members. Keep an alternative for a broken screen, low battery or scanning issue.

## Record outcomes, including exceptions

Record when an entry is granted or denied and why. For a manual override, capture who made it and the reason. This information helps resolve questions and improve the gym’s rules.

Review who can access these records and how long they need to be kept.

## Consider occupancy and exits

If your gym needs to know how many people are inside, open visits and exits must be represented accurately. Decide how to close a visit when someone forgets to record an exit or the process is interrupted.

If you use external hardware, test it with staff and real network conditions before relying on it to operate.

## Start with the most common flow

Map a regular arrival, an expired membership, exhausted passes and a correction made at reception. A clear process for these cases matters more than automating a device before deciding how the team should respond.
